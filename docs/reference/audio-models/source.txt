# TTS Models & Speech Synthesis Research Reference

## High-Performance & Commercial-Safe Models
Qwen3-TTS: 1.7B parameters, Apache 2.0, 10 languages, supports voice cloning and natural language direction.
Kokoro (v1.0): 82M parameters, Apache 2.0, English-only, extremely fast, runs on CPU.
Chatterbox: 350M parameters, MIT license, 23+ languages, supports zero-shot voice cloning.
Step Audio EditX: Apache 2.0, high-ranking on leaderboards, supports iterative token-space editing.
VoxCPM2: 2B parameters, Apache 2.0, 30 languages, native 48kHz output, runs on CPU via llama.cpp.
Orpheus: 150M–3B parameters, Apache 2.0, English, supports emotion tags and real-time streaming.
Dia (v2): 1.6B parameters, Apache 2.0, English, designed for multi-speaker dialogue and nonverbal cues.
CosyVoice2: 0.5B parameters, Apache 2.0, 9+ languages, ultra-low latency streaming (~150ms).
Zonos v0.1: Apache 2.0, 5 languages, high commercial safety.
Maya1: 3B parameters, Apache 2.0, English, built for expressive voice design with emotional tags.
NVIDIA Magpie TTS Multilingual: NVIDIA Open Model License, 12 languages, fixed speakers by design.
GLM-TTS: MIT weights, Chinese/English, high accuracy.
MetaVoice v1: Apache 2.0, English.
OpenVoice v2: MIT license, focused on voice conversion.

## Research, Non-Commercial, or Specialized Licenses
Fish Audio S2 Pro: 4.4B parameters, Research License (non-commercial), 80+ languages, high fidelity.
Voxtral TTS: 4B parameters, CC BY-NC 4.0, 9 languages, wins in controlled voice cloning benchmarks.
Breeze TTS 2: Research/non-commercial license, highest Elo rating on leaderboards, beats commercial models in quality.
Higgs Audio V3: Research/non-commercial, 102 languages, zero-shot cloning.
XTTS v2: CPML (non-commercial), 17 languages, mature zero-shot cloning tool.
F5-TTS: Weights CC-BY-NC, fast flow-matching for research-grade cloning.
Hume TADA: Open-source, zero hallucinations, supports long-form audio (up to 700s).
Sesame CSM: Conversational-grade TTS with natural turn-taking and audio watermarking.
OmniVoice: CC BY-NC, 600+ languages, zero-shot cloning.
OpenAudio S1-mini: CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0, 13 languages, multilingual research/demo.
VibeVoice: MIT (research use only/withdrawn), LLM-guided dialogue.

## Legacy, Lightweight, or Niche Models
Piper: Tiny (~15M parameters), MIT/GPL-3.0, 30–47 languages, CPU-only, ideal for Raspberry Pi/edge.
Bark: ~1B parameters, MIT, generates speech, music, and sound effects; creative/expressive.
MeloTTS: Lightweight, real-time, multilingual, strong CPU performance.
Mozilla TTS: Flexible framework, 18 languages, good for custom pipelines.
Coqui TTS: Broad toolkit, 7 languages, pre-trained voices.
Mimic 3: Fast, privacy-friendly, offline/embedded support.
ChatTTS: Optimized for conversational use cases and dialogue prosody.
StyleTTS 2: MIT + conditions, expressive style control.
eSpeak: Linux/Windows, 10+ languages, simple, medium quality.
MaryTTS: MacOS, 8 languages, high quality.
Vosk: Offline, embedded, low-power, 20+ languages.
Moonshine: On-device, English-focused, low-latency.
Tacotron / Tacotron2: Foundational autoregressive models.
WaveNet / WaveGlow: Generative audio models.
FastPitch / MixerTTS / RadTTS: NVIDIA NeMo pipeline components.
HiFiGAN / UnivNet: Vocoders often paired with TTS models.
VITS: Combines VAE and GAN for high-quality speech synthesis.
GlowTTS / GradTTS: Flow-based generation models.
Tortoise TTS: High quality but slow inference; known for cloning.
Parler TTS Mini: Smaller variant of Parler models.
Metavoice: 1.2B parameters, focused on voice agent consistency.
Supertonic: Small model (~120m params), efficient.
Gepard: Streaming-capable model.
Chatterbox-Nano: Tiny variant of Chatterbox.
Qwen3-TTS-0.6b: Smaller variant of Qwen3-TTS.
IndexTTS-2: Precise duration control, ideal for video dubbing.
Cartesia Sonic: Low-latency streaming (~90ms), purpose-built for agents.
LMNT: Low-latency (150–200ms), high concurrency support.
Fish Audio: API-integrated open-source options.
ElevenLabs Alternatives: Various models like Bark+RVC combinations used to rival commercial APIs.

## Key Considerations for Selection
Licensing: For commercial use, prioritize Apache 2.0 or MIT licensed models like Kokoro, Chatterbox, Qwen3-TTS, and CosyVoice2. Avoid CC BY-NC or Research licenses (e.g., Fish Audio, Breeze TTS 2) for products.
Hardware: Kokoro and Piper run on CPU. Chatterbox and CosyVoice2 fit on consumer GPUs (4–6GB VRAM). Voxtral and Fish Audio require 12–16GB+ VRAM.
Language: Fish Audio and OmniVoice support 80–600+ languages. Kokoro and Dia are English-only. Chatterbox supports 23+ languages. 
Voice Cloning: XTTS v2, Fish Audio, and Chatterbox support zero-shot cloning. Kokoro and Piper do not.
Latency: Voxtral (~90ms) and CosyVoice2 (~150ms) are optimized for real-time streaming. Bark and Tortoise are slower.
